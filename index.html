Scholar

Mohammed Farouk Nakmouche

Researcher, Huawei Technologies Canada Research Center — · Affiliated Scientist, Toronto Metropolitan University —

Mohammed Farouk Nakmouche (Member, IEEE) received the B.Sc. and M.Sc. degrees in telecommunication systems and networks engineering from Amar Telidji University of Laghouat, Algeria, in 2013 and 2015, respectively, and the Ph.D. degree in electrical engineering from École de technologie supérieure (ÉTS), Montréal, Canada, in 2025. From 2015 to 2020, he contributed to research and R&D projects across France, Turkey, Egypt, Taiwan, and Canada. He is currently a Researcher with Huawei Technologies Canada Research Center in Ottawa and an affiliated researcher at Toronto Metropolitan University. His doctoral dissertation, focused on machine-learning-aided design of additively manufactured ridge gap waveguide components, received the ÉTS Ph.D. Excellence Award.

Dr. Nakmouche’s research expertise includes machine-learning and optimization techniques for the analysis, synthesis, and design of microwave and millimeter-wave components and systems. His work spans inverse design, physics-informed neural networks, and surrogate modeling for antennas, filters, couplers, frequency-selective surfaces, and metasurfaces, with particular emphasis on substrate integrated waveguide, ridge and gap waveguide technologies, dielectric resonator antennas, MIMO arrays, lens/transmitarray antennas, and additive or flexible RF manufacturing. A central theme of his research is the development of practical end-to-end workflows linking electromagnetic theory, CST/HFSS/ADS simulation, AI-assisted synthesis, fabrication, and experimental validation.

Dr. Nakmouche serves as Chair of the IEEE MTT-S Student Ambassadors Program. He previously served as an IEEE MTT-S Graduate Student Ambassador and Co-Chair, contributing to student engagement and international collaboration across the microwave community. He was also selected as an IEEE Antennas and Propagation Society (AP-S) Young Professionals Ambassador for the year 2026. He previously served as Treasurer of the IEEE ÉTS Student Branch and remains active in IEEE student and young-professional initiatives. His research aims to bridge rigorous electromagnetic design with modern AI and manufacturable hardware for next-generation wireless, sensing, 6G, and Internet-of-Space systems.
