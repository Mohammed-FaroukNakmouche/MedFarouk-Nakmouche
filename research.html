Research

AI-assisted design and manufacturing of compact RF, microwave, and millimeter-wave hardware.

Future wireless and sensing platforms require smaller, lighter, more multifunctional RF front ends while the electromagnetic design space is becoming increasingly complex. Conventional trial-and-error optimization can be slow, strongly dependent on designer experience, and difficult to scale when geometry, materials, fabrication constraints, and multiple performance objectives are considered simultaneously.

My research addresses this challenge by combining electromagnetic modeling, data-driven and AI-assisted synthesis, advanced waveguide and antenna architectures, and manufacturable RF hardware. The goal is to move from manually tuned components toward design frameworks that can rapidly translate electrical specifications into practical geometries while preserving physical interpretability and experimental feasibility.

Research workflow

Target RF / EM specifications
→
Physics + AI-assisted synthesis
→
Manufacturable RF hardware

Research pillars

01

AI-assisted electromagnetic synthesis

Forward and inverse models, surrogate-assisted design, machine learning, and data-efficient strategies that map RF specifications to antenna and waveguide geometries with fewer full-wave iterations.

02

Antennas, MIMO & guided-wave components

Compact antennas, arrays, MIMO systems, SIW, PRGW, RGW, transitions, filters, and electromagnetic-bandgap concepts for microwave and millimeter-wave integration.

03

Advanced manufacturing & integration

3-D printing, low-cost fabrication, non-planar metallic structures, flexible concepts, antenna-in-package, and compact front-end integration for emerging wireless and space systems.

Current directions

Direct inverse synthesis

Replacing repeated geometry tuning with direct mappings from prescribed electromagnetic response to candidate designs, followed by full-wave verification.

Data-efficient learning

Reducing the cost of full-wave datasets through transfer learning, compact parameterizations, active data construction, and physics-aware normalization.

Non-conventional RF geometries

Exploring 3-D, irregular, reconfigurable, and additively manufactured structures that are difficult to design efficiently with classical templates alone.
